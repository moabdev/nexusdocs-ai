## About this FAQ

This FAQ translates common Velox Logistics policies and operational procedures into customer-friendly language. It is intended to help customers understand typical shipping, tracking, claim, and compensation scenarios.

When this FAQ conflicts with an applicable contract or governing ACTIVE policy, the authorized contract or governing policy controls.

## 1. My package says delivered, but I did not receive it. What should I do?

First, verify the delivery address and check with household members, reception, concierge, mailroom, and safe delivery locations. If the shipment is still missing, allow **24 hours from the recorded delivery timestamp** before a formal non-delivery investigation is opened. This waiting period follows the Tracking Operations Manual. If the shipment remains unlocated, Customer Experience can open a case and Operations may review proof-of-delivery and tracking evidence.

## 2. My tracking has not changed for several days. Is my package lost?

Not necessarily. A tracking gap does not automatically mean that a shipment is lost. Operations may review facility scans, route records, and transfer evidence. A shipment becomes eligible for potential loss classification only after the loss threshold established by the **ACTIVE Shipping Policy** has been reached.

## 3. How long do I have to file a shipping claim?

For current shipments governed by Shipping Policy v3.0, an eligible claim must generally be submitted within **15 calendar days** of the relevant delivery event, expected delivery date, or qualifying incident. Historical shipments may be governed by an earlier policy version, so the applicable policy date must be checked.

## 4. Does a potentially lost shipment automatically qualify for compensation?

No. Potential loss classification allows the investigation process to proceed, but it is not a final compensation decision. Eligibility, evidence, exclusions, documented value, and investigation results are considered before compensation is approved.

## 5. If my package is lost, do I automatically receive R$ 2,000?

No. **R$ 2,000 is the current maximum standard compensation ceiling**, not an automatic payment. Approved compensation is based on documented eligible loss. If the documented eligible value is lower than the ceiling, compensation is limited to that lower amount unless another authorized contract or declared-value arrangement applies.

## 6. What information do I need to open a claim?

Every claim should include the shipment tracking identifier, claimant name and contact information, a description of what happened, the date the issue was discovered, and the requested resolution. Depending on the case, Velox Logistics may also request invoices, receipts, proof of item value, photographs, recipient declarations, or other supporting evidence.

## 7. What evidence is needed for a damaged package?

When reasonably available, provide photographs of the external packaging, internal packaging, and damaged contents. Keep the shipment, contents, and packaging until the investigation is completed or Velox Logistics confirms that retention is no longer necessary.

## 8. How long does a claim investigation take?

Initial validation should normally occur within **2 business days** after submission. A standard investigation should normally be completed within **5 business days after all required evidence is available**. Complex cases involving cross-region tracing, fraud review, or third-party evidence may require up to **10 business days**.

## 9. Who investigates a missing shipment?

Customer Experience administers the customer case and communicates with the claimant. **Operations performs shipment tracing and evaluates operational evidence.** Finance may become involved when an authorized payment requires validation.

## 10. Why could my claim be denied?

Examples include filing outside the applicable deadline, insufficient evidence after a reasonable opportunity to provide it, prohibited contents, inadequate packaging that directly caused damage, unsupported claimed value, contractual exclusions, or confirmed successful delivery supported by sufficient evidence.

## 11. Can someone else receive my package?

Depending on the service and operating procedure, delivery may be completed to the named recipient or another authorized person at the destination. Available delivery evidence may include recipient information, timestamp, scan, route evidence, or other supported proof of delivery.

## 12. What does DELIVERY_EXCEPTION mean?

It means that something prevented normal delivery progression. Examples include an unavailable recipient, inaccessible destination, incorrect or incomplete address, weather disruption, route disruption, security restrictions, or a damaged shipment requiring operational review.

## 13. What does RETURN_TO_SENDER mean?

It means an authorized return flow has started. Common reasons include repeated failed delivery attempts, an invalid address, recipient refusal, or another authorized operational condition. A return-to-sender status does **not** automatically determine refund or compensation eligibility.

## 14. Can a delivered tracking status be wrong?

Yes. Tracking events represent the best available operational evidence, but an event may be corrected when a scan, timestamp, location, or delivery status is demonstrably inaccurate. Corrections should preserve an auditable record of the original event and the reason for the change.

## 15. Which policy should I use if different documents show different claim deadlines?

For a current shipment, use the **ACTIVE Shipping Policy** applicable to that shipment. Older SUPERSEDED policies are retained for historical questions and should not be treated as current rules. For example, older versions may show different deadlines because they governed earlier periods.
